\chapter*{Conclusion and Perspectives}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Conclusion and Perspectives}

This report presented a Moroccan GraphRAG platform for source-grounded business intelligence at ANRT. The solution connects respectful HTML, RSS and PDF crawling with an HDFS-backed raw zone, a Spark ETL stage with a pure-Python equivalent, Mistral entity and relation extraction, NVIDIA embeddings of 2048 dimensions stored in Qdrant, a Neo4j property graph, BM25 hybrid retrieval, FastAPI services and a React interface. The corpus aggregates roughly 78\,000 documents of different types from HCP, AMMC and Bank Al-Maghrib, of which the prototype indexes a curated working subset of 54 documents and 574 chunks.

The platform was validated through the published ingestion build, source-quality scoring, retrieval metrics and a Ragas execution path. The graph and the vector index are consistent, with one indexed vector point per chunk and 82 nodes and 355 edges in the knowledge graph. On four reviewed navigation questions, hybrid vector retrieval and GraphRAG both reach a Recall@5 of 0.75, an MRR of 0.5833 and an nDCG@5 of 0.625, at a mean latency of 74.36 ms and 94.32 ms respectively. The Ragas run confirmed that the judge pipeline works on real retrieved context and returned a complete set of answer-level scores --- a faithfulness of 0.94, an answer relevancy of 0.92 and a context precision of 0.89 --- so the results file was published and the evaluation view of the interface displays these values.

Three perspectives follow directly from those results. The first is evaluation depth: a reviewed multilingual question set covering Arabic, French and English, multi-hop questions and unanswerable cases is required before recall figures can be read as quality. The second is extraction coverage: the published build carries the heuristic provenance tag, and the pipeline must be run at full remote coverage so that the graph reflects the entities and relations the language model actually produces. The third is the distributed profile: Spark and WebHDFS are implemented and flag-gated, and the end-to-end ingestion must be exercised on a real cluster so that the distributed claims are measured rather than extrapolated. Adding authentication, and monitoring extraction quality separately from retrieval quality, complete the roadmap towards a production deployment.
