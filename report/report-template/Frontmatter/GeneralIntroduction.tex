\chapter*{Introduction}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Introduction}

Moroccan economic information is distributed across institutional websites, annual reports, RSS feeds, statistical tables and PDF publications. An analyst who needs a figure on the Moroccan economy must today open several portals, download heterogeneous files and reconstruct the answer manually. The difficulty is not only the volume of the material but its heterogeneity: the same information appears as an HTML page, as a scanned or native PDF report, as a syndicated feed entry and as a downloadable data file, in Arabic, French or English. A conventional search engine returns pages, not answers, and it rarely explains which source supports which statement.

This report presents a Graph Retrieval-Augmented Generation (GraphRAG) platform prepared for \textbf{l'Agence Nationale de Réglementation des Télécommunications (ANRT)}. The platform collects explicitly authorised Moroccan sources, stores them in a raw zone backed by HDFS when a cluster is available, cleans and deduplicates them with Spark, splits them into provenance-preserving chunks, indexes those chunks in a vector store and extracts the entities and relations that form a knowledge graph. A FastAPI service then answers questions by combining graph traversal with hybrid lexical and semantic retrieval, and it returns the evidence and the source URLs next to every answer.

The corpus currently aggregates approximately \textbf{78\,000 documents of different types} --- HTML pages, PDF reports, RSS/Atom entries and structured data files --- issued by HCP, AMMC and Bank Al-Maghrib. Because the value of such a system depends on the trustworthiness of its answers, the project also invests in measurement: the report presents a Ragas-based evaluation of answer faithfulness, answer relevancy and context precision, together with a retrieval benchmark that compares plain vector search with the graph-aware strategy.

The report is organised in four chapters followed by a conclusion. The first chapter describes the architecture, the deployment and the corpus. The second chapter details each main pipeline component, from the Scrapy crawlers to the GraphRAG service, including the HDFS raw zone, the Spark ETL stage, the chunking strategy, the BM25 hybrid retrieval and the models used with their embedding dimensions. The third chapter introduces Ragas and its metrics and presents the evaluation protocol. The fourth chapter reports the measured results. The conclusion summarises the contribution and the perspectives.
